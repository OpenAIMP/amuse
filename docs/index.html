Reading the screens — notes & lessons

Pre-market gap vs intraday momentum

The pre-market gap screen (9:00am ET) looks for overnight gaps ≥ 2% with pre-market volume ≥ 1.5× average. It only works before the open — its volume gate compares partial-day volume to a full-day average, which becomes nearly impossible by midday. An empty pre-market report after the open means the signal is stale, not that the market is quiet.

The intraday momentum screen (11am, 2pm ET) uses session-aware signals, rebuilt 2026-10-12 after the first version proved too narrow:

  • Opening-range breakout — and holding. The first version asked "is it piercing the 15-min range right now?" — but breakouts happen at 10am, not 1:15pm. Now: did it break the range at any point today, and is it still holding above (or below) it? A breakout that faded back inside the range is flagged separately.
  • VWAP — sustained position, not the cross moment. The first version only saw crosses in the last 30 minutes, so an 11am reclaim was invisible by 1:15pm. Now: what fraction of the session has price held above/below the developing VWAP? ≥ 70% one side counts as sustained.
  • Time-prorated volume. Today's volume so far vs the 20-day average scaled by elapsed session time — the fix for the pre-market screen's flaw. 1.5× at 11am means genuinely heavy trading, not an impossible bar.
  • Never a blank page. If nothing meets the strict gates, the strongest volume-plus-drift names still show as momentum watch rows.

RoR — return on risk

RoR = credit ÷ max loss. A 39.7% RoR on a $142 credit / $358 max-loss condor means: best case you keep $142, worst case you lose $358 — $0.40 of profit per $1 risked if it works. Higher RoR = more credit per dollar risked.

RoR is a ranking tool, not a gate and not a forecast. Nothing is rejected for low RoR; the tables are simply sorted by it. A high RoR never means a trade is likely to win — the edge in credit spreads is probability (price staying between the breakevens), not payout ratio.

Max loss + credit always equals the wing width ($358 + $142 = $500 on a 5-point wing). Max loss below credit would need RoR over 100% — the market never prices liquid spreads that way; if you see it, it's a bad quote.

Win%, EV$, Loss× — reading the odds

Three newer columns estimate what RoR alone doesn't say:

  • Win% — estimated probability the trade wins (keeps some credit). For a bull put: 1 − |short delta|. For an iron condor: (1 − |put delta|) × (1 − |call delta|). Delta approximates the market's pricing of exercise probability, so this is a model estimate, not a market fact. A 72% Win% means the options market prices roughly 72% odds the short legs expire worthless.
  • EV$ — expected dollar value: Win% × credit − (1 − Win%) × max loss. Positive EV means the trade is priced in your favor if the delta-based odds hold. Negative EV doesn't mean "don't trade" — it means you're paying for the defined risk, which is the point of spreads.
  • Loss× — loss multiple: max loss ÷ credit. A 2.5× means you risk $2.50 for every $1 of credit. Higher Loss× needs higher Win% to break even — the two columns read together.

Best / worst / optimal price ranges. Best case: price stays between the breakevens through expiration — you keep the full credit. Worst case: price blows through a long strike — you lose the max. Optimal: price pins near the short strikes, maximizing time decay. The breakeven column shows the exact boundaries.

Winners vs losers — how to read Win%

Every spread row now carries Win%, EV$, and Loss×:

  • High Win% (70%+) + positive EV$ — the market prices this in your favor. These are the "high chance of winning" candidates. Still defined-risk: a gap through the long strike loses the max.
  • High Win% + negative EV$ — you're paying for safety. Common on low-IV names; the trade is a hedge-like income position, not an edge.
  • Low Win% (<55%) + high RoR — lottery tickets. Big payout if right, but the odds say you'll lose more often than not. Size these tiny or skip.
  • Loss× above 4 — you're risking $4+ per $1 of credit. Needs Win% above 80% to break even on expectancy. Treat with suspicion.

Sort mentally by EV$ first, Win% second, RoR third. RoR ranks payout efficiency; EV$ ranks whether the odds justify the risk.

Reading the legs

First strike = short (sell), second = long (buy). 210/205P means sell the 210 put, buy the 205 put. 245/250C means sell the 245 call, buy the 250 call. The short strikes are where premium is collected — and what defines the profit zone via the breakevens.
